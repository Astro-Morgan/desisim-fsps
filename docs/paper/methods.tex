\subsection{Galaxy Continuum} \label{sec:galaxy-continuum}
Mirrors \texttt{src/demiurge/galaxy\_continuum/}. Produces the pure stellar
continuum for the galaxy mock path -- the \texttt{continuum} bucket's main
content. Dust attenuation is explicitly excluded from this channel
(\texttt{dust1}=\texttt{dust2}=0 in every FSPS call); it is the
responsibility of a separate, not-yet-built channel, following the 3-bucket
decomposition's own scope-separation convention.

\subsubsection{Star Formation History} \label{sec:sfh}
The star-formation history SFR($t$) is generated non-parametrically,
following the Dense Basis method of \citet{IyerGawiser2017} and
\citet{Iyer2019}. Three stellar-mass-formation-time quantiles
($t_{25}$, $t_{50}$, $t_{75}$, i.e.\ the lookback times at which 25\%,
50\%, and 75\% of the galaxy's total stellar mass has formed) are drawn
from a Dirichlet prior over the four time-gaps they divide $[0, t_{\rm
obs}]$ into (symmetric, concentration $\alpha=2$; inspired by, but not
independently verified against, the $\alpha=1$ per-bin sSFR-fraction prior
of \citet{IglesiasNavarro2024}, who apply a closely related Dense-Basis-
based method to simulation-based inference of SFHs from DESI-like spectra).
A Gaussian Process with a Mat\'ern-3/2 kernel is then conditioned to pass
exactly through these constraints (plus the fixed endpoints $F(0)=0$,
$F(t_{\rm obs})=1$) in cumulative-stellar-mass-fraction-vs-time space,
following \citet{Iyer2019}'s Section 2.1 method; SFR($t$) is obtained by
numerically differentiating the reconstructed curve, clipped non-negative
and rescaled to conserve the drawn total stellar mass exactly.

Two implementation departures from \citet{Iyer2019}, adopted for simplicity
(\texttt{sfh.py}'s module docstring): the reported stochasticity is the
Gaussian Process posterior \emph{mean} through randomly-drawn quantile
constraints, not a draw from the full posterior; and the smoothness/
burstiness length-scale hyperparameter's default range is not independently
verified against \citeauthor{Iyer2019}'s own calibrated value.

\subsubsection{Metallicity History} \label{sec:metallicity}
Rather than an independent free function of time, the metallicity history
$Z(t)$ is tied directly to the star formation history via the classical
closed-box chemical evolution relation of \citet{SearleSargent1972}:
\begin{equation}
    Z(t) = y \, \ln\left(\frac{1}{\mu(t)}\right), \qquad
    \mu(t) = 1 - \epsilon \, F(t),
\end{equation}
where $y$ is an effective nucleosynthetic yield, $\epsilon$ is a star-
formation efficiency (the fraction of the initial gas reservoir ultimately
locked into stars by $t_{\rm obs}$), and $F(t)$ is the same cumulative
stellar-mass fraction from Section~\ref{sec:sfh}. $Z(t)$ is monotonically
non-decreasing by construction. This is a deliberate first-pass
simplification: a more complete inflow/outflow-aware, SFH-regulated
chemical evolution model exists \citep{YinShenHao2023} but requires
machinery (a Kennicutt--Schmidt law, mass-loading-factor-parameterized
outflows) well beyond this pass's scope.

\subsubsection{Population inversion (SFH/Z generating-parameter grid)} \label{sec:population-inversion}

Real literature (age, metallicity) targets for disc/halo/elliptical
populations (Bensby, Feltzing \& Oey 2014; An et al. 2013; Xue et al. 2015;
Gallazzi et al. 2005; Thomas, Maraston, Bender \& Mendes de Oliveira 2005 --
pulled during planning, not yet wired into a query here, so not formally
cited in this section) are direct [Fe/H]/[Z/H]/age observables, none of
which map onto the $y$/$\epsilon$ closed-box parameterization above
directly. \texttt{galaxy\_continuum.population\_inversion} solves this by
inversion rather than by deriving a new formula: it builds a combined
dense-grid + Monte-Carlo point cloud over the SFH/metallicity generating
parameters (the quantile gap-fraction simplex -- parameterized via stick-
breaking over an ordinary cube for simple regular gridding, rather than the
raw 4-simplex -- plus the GP length-scale fraction, yield, and efficiency;
$6$ free dimensions, `total\_stellar\_mass` excluded since it cancels out of
both summaries below by construction), evaluates each point's resulting
mass-weighted age and metallicity, and looks up the nearest points to a
given target via a normalized-distance $k$-nearest-neighbor query -- a
regular grid guarantees boundary coverage (including corners the priors
rarely visit), Monte Carlo draws from the real registered priors supply
resolution where probability mass concentrates.

Two closed-form identities make the per-point evaluation cheap: integrating
the usual age definition by parts using $F(0)=0,\,F(t_{\rm obs})=1$ gives
mass-weighted age $= \int_0^{t_{\rm obs}} F(t)\,dt$ (no differentiation of
$F(t)$ needed); mass-weighted metallicity is
$\bar{Z} = \int Z(t)\,dF(t)$, computed directly as a trapezoidal integral
of $Z$ against $F$ itself. A real bug this module shipped with once (caught
by a unit test landing $\sim$2x off, not within noise, before being trusted):
$Z(t_{\rm obs}) = y\ln(1/(1-\epsilon))$ (the metallicity of only the LAST
gas enriched, exact since $F(t_{\rm obs})=1$ always) was wrongly assumed to
approximate $\bar{Z}$ (the mass-weighted average over ALL gas, the actually
relevant quantity for matching population-level literature targets) --
these are genuinely different numbers. $\bar{Z}$ in fact has its OWN exact,
SFH-shape-independent closed form: substituting $u=F(t)$ in
$\int Z(t)\,dF(t)$ leaves a definite integral in $u$ alone (since
$Z(t)=y\ln(1/(1-\epsilon F(t)))$ depends on $F(t)$ only), evaluating to
\begin{equation}
    \bar{Z} = y \left[1 + \frac{1-\epsilon}{\epsilon}\ln(1-\epsilon)\right],
\end{equation}
verified numerically against the real (shape-dependent machinery)
`evaluate\_population` to $\sim 10^{-4}$ relative precision.

This closed form for $\bar{Z}$ enables targeted sampling
(`build\_targeted\_samples`, `sample\_yield\_efficiency\_for\_target\_z`):
draw $\epsilon$ from its real registered prior, solve for the $y$ that
lands exactly on a target $\bar{Z}$, keep only draws whose implied $y$
falls within $y$'s registered support. Motivation, verified numerically
(2026-09-16): plain grid+Monte-Carlo sampling of $(y, \epsilon)$ induces a
$\bar{Z}$-distribution that is genuinely $\sim$2.6x sparser near a real
low-$\bar{Z}$ (halo-like) target than a real moderate-$\bar{Z}$
(elliptical-like) one at the same tolerance (out of $2\times10^6$ draws
from the real registered priors) -- a structural consequence of $\bar{Z}$
being a multiplicative function of two independently-distributed factors
(the level set near $\bar{Z}\to0$ occupies a genuinely smaller corner of
the $(y,\epsilon)$ rectangle than the level set near its upper reach), not
an artifact of this module's own sampling choices. Targeted sampling
directly fills whichever region is sparse rather than relying on more
blind draws eventually landing nearby. One honest limitation: the SFH-shape
draws paired with each targeted $(y,\epsilon)$ point still come from the
unconditional shape prior, so matching a target's AGE within the targeted
set is still down to luck, not yet similarly targeted.

\paragraph{Self-consistent extension (2026-09-17).} Andy's stated
preference, once Section~\ref{sec:metal-yield}'s metallicity-dependent
yield existed: use it here instead of the constant-yield closed form
above. \texttt{evaluate\_population\_self\_consistent}/
\texttt{build\_self\_consistent\_targeted\_samples}/
\texttt{build\_self\_consistent\_population\_target\_grid} are
the same idea, with two real problems this surfaced and fixed. First,
$\bar{Z}$ has no closed form once yield depends on the evolving $Z(t)$
itself, so the exact-targeting trick above becomes numerical bisection
over \texttt{yield\_scatter} instead of algebra -- monotonic in its effect on
$\bar{Z}$, so bisection converges reliably. Second, the self-consistent
evaluation measured $\sim$300x slower per call than the closed form
(RK4-stepping through the enrichment history vs. one formula, $\sim$53ms
vs. $\sim$0.17ms) -- addressed by a coarser step grid (30 steps instead
of 200; checked directly, $<$5\% deviation, negligible next to the
35--70\% astrophysical scatter Section~\ref{sec:metal-yield} already
models) and by vectorizing the RK4 stepper across many points' bisection
trials at once instead of one Python-level call per point -- together,
$\sim$100x faster, confirmed by direct benchmark. AGE-targeting was also
added (an improvement over the closed-box version's "down to luck"
limitation): a large, cheap candidate pool (checking a shape's age needs
no ODE integration, only the SFH reconstruction) is kept to whichever
points are CLOSEST to the target age, rather than accepted within a fixed
window (which inherits any local asymmetry in the shape prior's own age
distribution) -- verified directly on the three representative literature
targets (thin disc, halo, massive elliptical): metallicity lands within
$<$1\% of target in every case, age within $\lesssim$1 Gyr, with the pool
size growing automatically for genuinely rare target ages (checked on the
12~Gyr halo case: closing the age bias from $-0.47$ to $-0.01$~Gyr needed
growing the pool from 4,000 to 60,000 candidates, still under 10 seconds).
Reachability is checked BEFORE nearest-in-age selection, not after --
checking in the other order under-fills the requested batch size
unpredictably.

\subsubsection{Initial Mass Function} \label{sec:imf}
The full theory of a time-varying, galaxy-wide stellar initial mass
function (the Integrated Galaxy-wide IMF, IGIMF: \citealt{KroupaWeidner2003};
modern formulation \citealt{Jerabkova2018}) derives the galaxy-wide IMF by
integrating a per-embedded-cluster stellar IMF against an embedded-cluster
mass function whose slope depends on the instantaneous star-formation rate,
with the maximum stellar mass per cluster set by a transcendental
mass-normalization condition. This is deliberately \emph{not} implemented
here, in favor of a much simpler treatment prioritizing a working,
observationally-testable channel over complete IMF physics.

What \emph{is} implemented is the metallicity-dependent shift of the
canonical \citet{Kroupa2001} IMF's low-mass (0.08--0.5~$M_\odot$) and
intermediate-mass (0.5--1~$M_\odot$) power-law slopes,
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_{1,2}(Z) = \alpha_{1,2}^{\rm canonical} + 0.5\,[M/H],
\end{equation}
with $[M/H] = \log_{10}(Z/Z_\odot)$ (using $Z_\odot = 0.0142$,
\citealt{Asplund2009}, matching the MIST isochrones used throughout),
$\alpha_1^{\rm canonical}=1.3$, $\alpha_2^{\rm canonical}=2.3$
\citep{Kroupa2001}. This relation was verified directly against the source
code of \texttt{galIMF} \citep{YanJerabkovaKroupa2017,
YanJerabkovaKroupaVazdekis2019}, the public reference implementation of the
IGIMF framework, rather than inferred from the papers' text alone. The
high-mass slope $\alpha_3$ -- the one that actually controls how many
massive, metal-producing stars form -- is ALSO now metallicity-dependent
(added 2026-09-16, PI direction), via \citet{RecchiEtAl2014} eq. 6 ("mild"
model, galIMF's own \texttt{'R14'} option, independently confirmed against
galIMF's source):
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_3(Z) = \alpha_3^{\rm canonical} + 0.0572\,[{\rm Fe/H}],
\end{equation}
a genuine metallicity-ONLY simplification of \citet{MarksEtAl2012}'s full
density+metallicity joint relation, obtained by fixing the embedded-cluster
density at a constant fiducial value inside it -- avoiding the cluster-
mass-function integration this project still does not implement. The
SFR-driven term of the full IGIMF relation remains uncaptured (still a real
gap, now smaller than before). A fuller physical treatment, deriving the
IMF's characteristic mass from gas thermodynamics (dust-gas coupling,
metal-line cooling, radiative feedback) rather than an empirical SFR/$Z$
scaling \citep{ShardaKrumholz2022}, and potentially incorporating AGN
radiative feedback as an additional regulating mechanism (no literature
precedent identified for the latter -- would be a genuine extension of this
project's own, not a citation), remains deferred future work.

\subsubsection{Self-consistent metallicity-dependent chemical yield} \label{sec:metal-yield}

Closes a real gap identified by Andy (2026-09-16): a top-heavier IMF at low
$Z$ should mean more massive, metal-producing stars form per unit mass
formed, hence a higher effective chemical yield -- previously the dynamic-
IMF mode (Section~\ref{sec:imf}) and the closed-box enrichment model
(Section~\ref{sec:metallicity}) shared $Z(t)$ as an input/output with no
actual feedback between them. \texttt{galaxy\_continuum.metal\_yield}
derives a real $\mathrm{yield}(Z)$ from actual core-collapse supernova
nucleosynthesis data (\citealt{LimongiChieffi2018}, VizieR/CDS
J/ApJS/237/13, downloaded and parsed directly -- chosen specifically
because it is structurally the same kind of per-progenitor-mass isotopic
yield table as \citet{WooseleyWeaver1995} (which \citealt{RecchiKroupa2015}
itself uses for its own closely related calculation, but which proved
impossible to extract cleanly from an OCR'd scan), making it the most
consistent available substitute) integrated against the Kroupa IMF with
$\alpha_{1,2,3}(Z)$ above.

\textbf{Validation}: at the canonical (untouched Kroupa 2001) IMF using the
solar-metallicity table, the computed effective yield (0.0098) matches
\citet{RecchiKroupa2015}'s own stated "typical" canonical-IMF value (0.01,
their invariant-IMF comparison) to within 2\%, and is the same order of
magnitude as \citet{KobayashiEtAl2020}'s independently-computed IMF-
integrated yield (their Table 3, $\sim$0.015) via a completely different
table and methodology. Using each real metallicity's OWN matching L\&C18
table (not the solar table reused everywhere -- an earlier, less complete
pass through this calculation used only the solar table and overstated the
resulting low-$Z$ yield boost) together with $\alpha_{1,2,3}(Z)$ gives four
real, self-consistent $([{\rm Fe/H}], \mathrm{yield})$ anchors spanning
$[{\rm Fe/H}]=0$ to $-3$: yield rises smoothly from 0.0098 to 0.0149 (a
real $\sim$1.5x increase; a lower-metallicity star's own weaker winds/
different pre-SN structure partially counteracts, but does not cancel, the
IMF-driven boost).

\textbf{Central curve and spread}: a monotone cubic Hermite spline
(hand-rolled Fritsch--Carlson/PCHIP algorithm -- no \texttt{scipy}, per this
project's NumPy-only hard dependency) through the 4 real anchors, held flat
outside $[{\rm Fe/H}]\in[-3,0]$ (the same "hold flat beyond the table's own
coverage" convention already used in
Sections~\ref{sec:dissociation-radius}/\ref{sec:population-inversion}, not
a new rule). The registered scatter,
\texttt{galaxy\_continuum.metallicity.yield\_scatter} $\sim
\mathcal{N}(0,1)$, is drawn once per mock and applied as
$\mathrm{yield}(Z)\cdot(1+\sigma_{\rm rel}(Z)\cdot\mathrm{yield\_scatter})$,
floored at 5\% of the mean to keep the ODE below well-posed. Per PI
direction, $\sigma_{\rm rel}(Z)$ reflects genuinely different confidence by
region: 35\% inside L\&C18's real tabulated coverage (grounded in the real
cross-methodology spread found even at the best-anchored solar point --
this project's own 0.0098 vs. \citeauthor{KobayashiEtAl2020}'s $\sim$0.015
differ by $\sim$53\%), 70\% outside it (pure extrapolation, no real
per-mass data at all).

\textbf{The self-consistency ODE}: $Z(t)$ no longer has a closed form once
yield depends on the evolving $Z(t)$ itself.
\citet{RecchiKroupa2015} solve the general (SFR-and-$Z$-dependent) version
of this problem, but it is genuinely implicit there (their own words:
"this system of equations is indeed implicit... an implicit procedure must
be employed") because their SFR and $Z$ depend on each other circularly.
This project's IMF treatment has no SFR-dependence at all (a pre-existing,
documented scope limit, not added for this feature) -- dropping that term
removes the circularity entirely, leaving a plain initial-value ODE,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{dZ}{d\mu} = -\frac{\mathrm{yield}_{\rm eff}(Z)}{\mu}, \qquad Z(\mu{=}1)=0,
\end{equation}
integrated forward via a hand-rolled RK4 stepper (again, no \texttt{scipy})
over the same $\mu(t)$ grid Section~\ref{sec:sfh} already produces.
Verified to reproduce the OLD closed-box relation $Z=y_0\ln(1/\mu)$ exactly
for a synthetic constant-yield right-hand side, isolated from the real
(genuinely Z-varying) $\mathrm{yield}(Z)$ curve, before being trusted.

\textbf{Deliberately NOT wired into the default generation path}
(Section~\ref{sec:metallicity}'s \texttt{draw\_metallicity}, or
Section~\ref{sec:population-inversion}'s inversion grid) -- that
section's own \texttt{mass\_weighted\_z\_closed\_form} (and the whole
targeted-sampling mechanism built on it) relies on $Z(t)$ having a closed
form independent of SFH shape, true for the OLD constant-yield relation
but not true once yield depends on the evolving $Z(t)$ itself. Wiring this
in as the default would mean reworking those closed-form shortcuts into
numerical ones -- a real, separate integration decision, not made in this
pass. Full parameter list in \texttt{src/demiurge/parameters/registry.py}
(\texttt{galaxy\_continuum.metallicity.yield\_scatter}).

\subsubsection{Spectral Synthesis}
Given SFR($t$), $Z(t)$, and (in the ``dynamic'' IMF mode) $\alpha_{1,2}(Z(t))$
from the sections above, the composite stellar spectrum is synthesized via
the Flexible Stellar Population Synthesis code \citep[FSPS;][]{Conroy2009,
Conroy2010}, using the MIST isochrones and the high-resolution C3K
spectral library. Two numerically distinct implementations are provided,
verified to agree (root-mean-square relative flux deviation
$\lesssim$ a few percent on held-out draws; see \texttt{demiurge}'s test
suite):

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{fsps\_direct}: the star-formation history is split into
    several contiguous time segments, each assigned its own
    SFR-weighted mean metallicity (and, in dynamic mode, IMF slopes); FSPS
    is called once per segment via its tabulated-SFH mode, and the
    resulting spectra are summed (valid since stellar population spectra
    are linear in formed mass at fixed age, metallicity, and IMF). This is
    the slower (order 10--25~s per FSPS call) but always-available
    implementation, used as the validation reference for the alternative
    below.
    \item \textbf{pretabulated} (default): exploits the empirical finding
    that FSPS caches its full native age-grid response for a given
    (metallicity, IMF) setting on first evaluation, making subsequent
    age queries at that same setting orders of magnitude faster than a new
    setting. A modest, log-spaced grid of FSPS's full age response at
    fixed metallicity values (log-spaced: empirically substantially more
    accurate per grid point than linear spacing, since realized $Z(t)$
    histories spend most of their dynamic range at low $Z$) is
    precomputed once, offline, and shipped as static data; spectra are
    then reconstructed via bilinear interpolation (in log-age and log-$Z$)
    of that grid, implemented in both NumPy and PyTorch (auto-detecting
    CUDA), with no further calls to FSPS. This reduces per-object synthesis
    time from order 10~s to order 1--10~ms.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Monte Carlo age averaging.} FSPS's isochrone response near
certain rapid evolutionary transitions (e.g.\ $\sim$60--71~Myr at
$Z\!\sim\!0.006$) can change by many orders of magnitude in the EUV/He$^+$-
ionizing tail ($\sim$100--300\AA) between two nearby ages, because that
flux is dominated by a vanishingly small number of extremely short-lived
hot post-main-sequence stars -- whether the isochrone's finite stellar-mass
grid happens to place one at a given exact age is essentially stochastic,
confirmed to be a genuine property of the FSPS/MIST isochrone response
itself (reproduced by querying the official code directly at densely
spaced ages), not an artifact of either implementation above. This jump is
physical and is not itself a problem; the actual defect is that a
\emph{single} raw sample at each grid age bakes one arbitrary,
non-reproducible realization of that stochastic response permanently into
the shipped \textbf{pretabulated} grid. Each of the grid's age points is
therefore built as the mean of $K=25$ FSPS evaluations at ages drawn
uniformly from a small window (half-width equal to half the native age
grid's own spacing) around that target age, estimating the smooth,
reproducible \emph{expectation} of the stochastic response rather than one
arbitrary draw of it (\texttt{scripts/build\_galaxy\_continuum\_ssp\_grid.py}).
\textbf{fsps\_direct} is unaffected by default -- a real narrow recent-burst
segment legitimately can show this stochastic variance, and hiding it would
misrepresent the isochrone -- but accepts an opt-in \texttt{n\_mc\_samples}
argument that applies the analogous averaging to its own per-segment
tabulated-SFH calls (each trial re-evaluated with the segment's whole time
support shifted by a small random $\Delta t$, drawn the same way, holding
the observation time fixed), for callers who want the population
expectation rather than one stochastic realization -- e.g.\ for a fair
comparison against the now-smoothed pretabulated grid.

\subsection{Quasar Continuum} \label{sec:quasar-continuum}
Mirrors \texttt{src/demiurge/quasar\_continuum/}. Produces the AGN
accretion-disk continuum -- the additive AGN contribution to the
\texttt{continuum} bucket, alongside Section~\ref{sec:galaxy-continuum}'s
stellar contribution. Blending the two into one composite continuum, and
any AGN-local reddening applied before that blend, is a separate,
not-yet-built channel.

The physical model is AGNSED \citep{KubotaDone2018}, in full (not its own
restricted special case QSOSED): an outer standard \citet{NovikovThorne1973}
relativistic disc from $R_{\rm out}$ to $R_{\rm warm}$, an optically-thick
warm Comptonising region from $R_{\rm warm}$ to $R_{\rm hot}$ that produces
the soft X-ray excess, and a hot, optically-thin corona from $R_{\rm hot}$
to the innermost stable circular orbit that produces the hard X-ray
power-law tail, all tied together self-consistently by requiring the
warm/hot regions' dissipated and reprocessed luminosities to balance against
the same underlying Novikov-Thorne emissivity profile. The disc self-gravity
radius $R_{\rm out}$ follows \citet{LaorNetzer1989}; the warm and hot-corona
spectral shapes are computed with the thermal-Comptonisation (`nthcomp')
model of \citet{ZdziarskiJohnsonMagdziarz1996} as extended by
\citet{ZyckiDoneSmith1999}, using a from-scratch Python translation of the
underlying Kompaneets-equation solve that is a light cleanup of
\citet{Thomas2016}'s independently-published translation of the same
Fortran subroutine (their OXAF model's supplementary code), since HEASARC
does not distribute this specific piece (`donthcomp.f') standalone -- it is
a core XSPEC built-in, not a community-contributed local model the way
AGNSED/QSOSED themselves are.

This implementation is a genuine reimplementation of the physics, not a
runtime wrapper around simqso or any other external quasar-modeling
package (the same dependency-minimization posture as
Section~\ref{sec:galaxy-continuum}'s pretabulated grid), and was validated
directly against the official, HEASARC-distributed Fortran source
(\texttt{agnsed.f}/\texttt{qsosed.f}, ``now in the Heasoft release as of
Xspec version 12.11'' per that source's own README) compiled and run
unmodified (\texttt{gfortran -std=legacy}), rather than against any
third-party port: the solved radial structure ($R_{\rm hot}$, $R_{\rm
warm}$, $R_{\rm out}$, $\Gamma_{\rm hot}$, $L_{\rm diss,hot}$, $L_{\rm hot}$)
reproduces the compiled reference to four or more decimal places, including
for \citet{KubotaDone2018}'s own three individually-fitted AGN (NGC~5548,
Mrk~509, PG~1115+407, their Table~2); total energy conservation across the
disc/warm/hot partition (the summed luminosity must equal $\dot m
L_{\rm Edd}$) holds to within a few percent (see below for a correction
applied here that brought this down from an initially measured
$\sim$9--13\%); and the full synthesized spectral shape and absolute
normalization together reproduce \citet{KubotaDone2018}'s own published
Figure~9 ($M=10^8\,M_\odot$, $\dot m=0.05$ and $0.5$, evaluated as a flux
at their stated fiducial 100~Mpc distance and $i=45^\circ$ inclination) to
within $\sim$2\% once compared on genuinely equal footing. One
precisely-diagnosed bug was found in the official Fortran's own diagnostic
print statements along the way (a variable, already correctly repurposed
as a flux for the final output spectrum's own normalization, gets reused a
few lines later in a temperature print statement that expects a true
luminosity instead) -- confirmed by exactly reproducing the Fortran's
printed value when the same variable reuse is deliberately replicated; it
does not affect the model's actual physics or this implementation, which
always uses the correct, undivided luminosity.

A second, more consequential issue was found and, unlike the print-only
bug above, corrected in this implementation: with reprocessing enabled,
the official Fortran's own output spectrum double-counts a fraction of the
disc/warm zones' photon energy. AGNSED's hot corona intercepts a
geometric, view-factor-weighted fraction of each outer annulus's photons
as Comptonisation seed photons, and its own luminosity $L_{\rm hot}$
(Eq.~1 of \citealt{KubotaDone2018}) is normalized to include that
intercepted energy -- but the disc/warm zones' own synthesized spectra
were never reduced by the same intercepted fraction, so that energy is
emitted in full by the disc/warm zones \emph{and} re-emitted, Comptonised,
by the corona. This was confirmed present in the compiled reference
Fortran itself (not introduced by this reimplementation, whose geometry
solve reproduces \texttt{agnsed.f}/\texttt{qsosed.f}'s own intercepted-flux
term, \texttt{seeddis}, to the same four-or-more-decimal-place accuracy
quoted above), and is corrected here by multiplying each disc/warm
annulus's emitted luminosity by one minus that same intercepted fraction
before spectral synthesis, so the energy is counted once rather than
twice. This is a deliberate departure from the reference Fortran's output
\emph{spectrum} (its solved geometry/energetics are untouched and remain
exact to four-plus decimal places): post-correction, total luminosity
across the three zones lands within a few percent of $\dot m L_{\rm Edd}$
rather than the $\sim$9--13\% excess measured before the fix, and the
Figure~9 comparison above is quoted post-correction.

Unlike the reference Fortran (and unlike a third-party Python port of
QSOSED evaluated, and rejected as a runtime dependency, before this module
was written -- see project history), the synthesized spectrum is built
entirely in specific-luminosity space, with no assumed observer distance
at any point, so that its output matches
Section~\ref{sec:galaxy-continuum}'s own convention directly: $L_\nu$
[$L_\odot\,\mathrm{Hz}^{-1}$] by default, $L_\lambda$
[$L_\odot\,\mathrm{\AA}^{-1}$] via the same conversion used there. That
third-party port's normalization bug -- silently omitting the inclination
parameter entirely, compounded with a further disc-specific geometric
error -- was only discovered because this project's practice is to
validate a reimplementation against a real compiled or independently
published reference before trusting its output, not because of any flaw
specific to that one package.

Several of the parameters below are ones QSOSED (Kubota \& Done's own
simplified variant of AGNSED) pins at a single ``typical'' value for
fitting faint, high-redshift quasars; here they are drawn as genuine Tier-2
NPE-parameters instead, because \citet{KubotaDone2018} themselves report
the measured population scatter around that typical value, not because the
fixed value itself was wrong. In particular, the hot-corona dissipated
luminosity fraction $L_{\rm diss,hot}/L_{\rm Edd}$ (QSOSED's hardcoded
$0.02$) becomes a Tier-2 parameter with a real citable range
\citep[$0.02$--$0.04$ across their three fitted AGN, consistent with the
factor 2--3 scatter across the larger sample of][]{JinWardDone2012a}; the
outer radius $R_{\rm hot}$ that this fraction implies is then solved for
via the same forward-accumulation-until-threshold procedure QSOSED itself
uses internally (walk outward from the ISCO, accumulate the disc-luminosity
integral, and freeze $R_{\rm hot}$ the moment the running total first
reaches the target), generalized from QSOSED's own hardcoded single target
to any drawn value. The inclination prior ($\cos i \in [0, 1]$, the full
isotropic range) follows the standard AGN unification argument
\citep{Antonucci1993, Netzer2015} that the torus/disc symmetry axis is
isotropically oriented across the general AGN population, with type-1 vs.
type-2 classification an emergent consequence of whether a given sightline
happens to intercept the torus -- not a precondition on which systems get
generated. (An earlier version of this prior restricted $\cos i \in [0.5,
1]$ as a workaround for not yet having any torus-obscuration model; that
restriction was lifted once \texttt{quasar\_continuum.torus\_reddening}
began modeling obscuration explicitly as a deterministic consequence of
this same draw -- see below.) The full parameter list, tiers, and citations
are catalogued in \texttt{src/demiurge/parameters/registry.py}
(\texttt{quasar\_continuum.agnsed.*}).

\subsection{Torus-local reddening}
\label{sec:torus-reddening}

Obscuration is not an independent free draw -- it is a deterministic
consequence of the same $\cos i$ value already drawn for the disc/warm
Lambertian projection above. \texttt{quasar\_continuum.torus\_reddening}
draws a covering-angle cosine $\cos\theta_{\rm oc}$ (representing the
torus's half-opening angle, measured from the pole) independently of
$\cos i$; the sightline intercepts the torus, and torus-local reddening
applies, iff $\cos i < \cos\theta_{\rm oc}$. This mirrors how the disc/warm
Lambertian factor itself is already computed inside the physics module
rather than via any sampler-level correlation -- \texttt{PriorSampler} only
supports independent draws, and this gate is ordinary physics-module logic
operating on two already-independently-drawn values, not a departure from
that constraint. $\cos\theta_{\rm oc}$ is drawn from
$\mathrm{Uniform}(0.13, 0.47)$, approximating the population scatter
\citet{EzhikodeEtAl2017} measure directly (mean covering factor
$f_c = 0.30 \pm 0.17$ across 51 local type-1 AGN).

The reddening curve itself, when intercepted, is the flat, bump-free
power-law family
\begin{equation}
    k(\lambda) = \theta_0 (\lambda/\lambda_V)^{-\theta_1}
\end{equation}
($\lambda_V = 5500\,\mathrm{\AA}$; $\theta_2$, the UV-bump amplitude, and
$\theta_3$, a grey floor, both fixed at zero -- not drawn), with
transmission $T(\lambda) = 10^{-0.4 k(\lambda)}$. This curve is a UV/
optical extinction law, not calibrated against any real measurement into
the EUV/X-ray; \texttt{quasar\_continuum}'s own native grid extends to
$\sim$0.06\,\AA{}, and naively extrapolating the power-law term that far
blueward diverges (found via visual verification of the blended
composite, 2026-09-11: a real registered $\theta_1$ draw produced
$k\sim330$, $T\sim10^{-133}$ at 100\,\AA{}). Below $\lambda=912\,
\mathrm{\AA}$ (the Lyman limit -- used here only as a recognizable UV
boundary, not because dust-grain physics has any actual feature there:
the real Lyman-limit discontinuity belongs to neutral-hydrogen
photoionization, a distinct, deliberately not-yet-built mechanism), $k$
follows a QUADRATIC in $\ln\lambda$,
\begin{equation}
    \ln k(\lambda) = \ln k(\lambda_{\rm floor}) - \theta_1 (\ln\lambda - \ln\lambda_{\rm floor})
                       + \theta_{\rm euv} (\ln\lambda - \ln\lambda_{\rm floor})^2,
\end{equation}
with a new free parameter $\theta_{\rm euv}$
(\texttt{euv\_curvature}) -- since no real physical treatment of this
regime exists, per PI direction (2026-09-13) this is represented as a
genuine Tier-3 NPE-parameter rather than a fixed extrapolation rule. By
construction, value AND slope match the real $\theta_1$ curve exactly at
$\lambda_{\rm floor}$ for ANY $\theta_{\rm euv}$ (verified numerically,
not merely derived): $\theta_{\rm euv}=0$ exactly continues the plain
power law (unbounded, not excluded); $\theta_{\rm euv}<0$ turns the curve
over toward transparency at shorter wavelengths; $\theta_{\rm euv}>0$
diverges faster. $\theta_{\rm euv} \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(-2.0, 0.1)$ (PI
direction), Tier 3, drawn independently for torus- and host-disk-local
reddening (physically distinct dust, no assumed correlation). Three
earlier attempts -- clamping transmission to exactly 1 below the floor
(produced a real, visually obvious step, since the curve's own raw value
was already far from 1 by the time the floor engaged), smoothly
saturating $k$ at a fixed but ungrounded ceiling constant, and holding $k$
flat at its boundary value (perfectly continuous, but a fixed
deterministic rule with no free parameter, inconsistent with this
project's own convention that unconstrained physics gets a real Tier-3
prior) -- were each tried, found wanting, and discarded before this one.
The same mechanism applies to host-disk-local reddening below.
\citet{GaskellEtAl2004} find AGN nuclear reddening curves significantly
flatter in the UV than the local ISM/SMC, with no 2175\,\AA\ bump, and
specifically "very flat" for their least host-contaminated (radio-loud)
sample -- motivating both the bump-free simplification and the narrow
$\theta_1 \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(0, 0.8)$ slope prior (well below the
general-reach $[0,2]$ range a non-AGN-specific curve family would use).
Their own finding that host-contaminated (radio-quiet) curves are somewhat
steeper is consistent with -- and motivates keeping distinct from --
host-disk-local reddening (a separate, not-yet-built channel), rather than
folding both effects into one shared draw. $\theta_0$ (the amplitude when
intercepted) has no dedicated covering-factor-to-reddening calibration yet
and is drawn from a MAGIC $\mathrm{Uniform}(0.1, 3.0)$ bracket pending one.
Full parameter list in \texttt{src/demiurge/parameters/registry.py}
(\texttt{quasar\_continuum.torus\_reddening.*}).

\subsection{Host-disk-local reddening}
\label{sec:host-disk-reddening}

A physically distinct screen from Section~\ref{sec:torus-reddening}'s
compact nuclear torus: the host galaxy's own disk material along the
specific nuclear sightline to the AGN. \texttt{quasar\_continuum.host\_disk\_reddening}
draws its own inclination, $\cos i_{\rm disk} \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(0, 1)$,
completely independently of the torus's $\cos i$ --
\citet{HopkinsEtAl2012} find the AGN/torus symmetry axis is not correlated
with the host galaxy's own large-scale disk axis (independent
angular-momentum transport at very different physical scales), so
independent isotropic draws for both is not a simplifying shortcut, it is
what the actual 3D geometry gives once random relative alignment between
the two axes is accounted for.

Unlike the torus's hard geometric threshold, this is a smooth path-length
effect: a foreground dust layer the sightline crosses exiting the host
disk, with the face-on amplitude $A_{V,\rm faceon}$ scaled by a capped
secant path-length factor, $\min(1/\cos i_{\rm disk},\,5)$ -- the standard
geometric shape real disk radiative-transfer attenuation models (e.g.
\citealt{TuffsEtAl2004}) qualitatively saturate toward rather than
literally diverge at $i \to 90^\circ$ (not fit to their tables here, only
their qualitative shape, via the hard cap). $A_{V,\rm faceon}$ itself is
drawn from a \texttt{ZeroInflated} prior ($p_{\rm zero}=0.3$, base
$\mathrm{LogUniform}(0.05, 2.0)$, both MAGIC): a continuously-drawn $\cos
i_{\rm disk}$ has probability exactly zero of landing at perfect face-on,
so a purely smooth function of inclination alone cannot represent "this
host is genuinely dust-free" -- a real, independent-of-inclination
population fact this project's other continuous families (LogUniform,
LogNormal) structurally exclude. The reddening curve reuses
$k(\lambda) = \theta_0 (\lambda/\lambda_V)^{-\theta_1}$ from
Section~\ref{sec:torus-reddening} (same $912\,\mathrm{\AA}$ floor) with
$\theta_0 = A_{V,\rm faceon} \times$ the path-length factor, and a wider
$\theta_1 \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(0, 1.3)$ slope range than the torus's --
this is ordinary host-galaxy ISM dust, not AGN-processed nuclear dust, so
it is not restricted to the torus's narrower flat/SMC-like family. This
range was revised 2026-09-11: an earlier $\mathrm{Uniform}(0,2)$ (reused
wholesale from \texttt{main}'s general-reach "fit any real curve"
convention, appropriate for fitting empirical curves over their own
measured range but never checked against extrapolating the same slope as
a bare power law all the way to the Lyman limit) let a real
$\theta_1=1.897$ draw -- steeper than the steepest well-established Local
Group extinction curve ever measured -- produce a $\sim$99\% flux
discontinuity at the floor (found via visual verification of the blended
composite). \citet{PrevotEtAl1984} measure the SMC bar's far-UV power-law
index at $n\sim1.2$; $\mathrm{Uniform}(0, 1.3)$ keeps a small margin above
that real ceiling rather than an unchecked generic bracket, and is now
Tier 2 rather than MAGIC. $\theta_2$ (UV bump) and $\theta_3$ (grey
floor) are fixed at zero for this first pass, but -- unlike the torus case
-- this is a deliberate scope-narrowing choice, not a bump-free physical
finding (ordinary ISM dust genuinely can show a real 2175\,\AA\ bump);
revisit if a specific need arises. Full parameter list in
\texttt{src/demiurge/parameters/registry.py}
(\texttt{quasar\_continuum.host\_disk\_reddening.*}).

\subsection{AGN-proximity dust-sublimation radius}
\label{sec:dissociation-radius}

\texttt{quasar\_continuum.dissociation\_radius} computes the distance
inside which the AGN's own radiation field is harsh enough to sublimate
dust grains -- not yet consumed downstream (it feeds the not-yet-built
CLOUDY-based ionization emulator's metallicity self-consistency
treatment), registered ahead of that consumer per this project's usual
scaffolding-ahead-of-content practice. \citet{Barvainis1987}'s classical
radiative-equilibrium result,
\begin{equation}
    R_{\rm sub} = K \sqrt{L_{\rm UV} / 10^{46}\,{\rm erg\,s^{-1}}}\ \ [{\rm pc}],
\end{equation}
absorbs the dust sublimation temperature/grain-size dependence
($(T_{\rm sub}/1500\,{\rm K})^{-2.8}(a/0.05\,\mu{\rm m})^{-0.5}$ in
Barvainis's own formula) into a single prefactor $K$, drawn from
$\mathrm{Uniform}(0.4, 1.3)\,{\rm pc}$ -- Barvainis's own quoted range
across the grain assumptions his paper considers, genuine (if uncertain)
grain physics rather than an invented bracket, hence Tier 2.

The inclination ($\cos i$) dependence is deliberately NOT an imported
empirical correction. Two independent, real mechanisms are documented in
the literature for why observed sublimation/torus radii run shallower
with luminosity than Barvainis's isotropic $L^{1/2}$ law predicts:
anisotropic disc illumination (the disc radiates less toward the equator
than the pole, present at any accretion rate; \citealt{KawaguchiMori2010,
KawaguchiMori2011,MinezakiEtAl2019}) and slim-disc self-shadowing (at high
Eddington ratio the puffed-up inner disc advects energy inward rather
than radiating it, reducing escaping power independent of viewing angle;
\citealt{WangEtAl2014}, applied to observed torus size-luminosity data by
\citealt{ChenEtAl2023}). The first mechanism is not imported here because
\texttt{quasar\_continuum.continuum} already applies
$\cos i_{\rm scale} = \cos i / 0.5$ (Section~\ref{sec:quasar-continuum}'s
Lambertian disc/warm geometry) to the disc+warm luminosity before this
module ever sees it -- bolting a population-averaged anisotropy
correction on top of luminosity already reduced for the same physical
reason would double-count the same effect. Instead, $L_{\rm UV}$ is
computed directly from AGNSED's own already-cosi-scaled disc+warm
photon-rate output (excluding the isotropic hot-corona/X-ray tail, which
is not the optical/UV continuum that heats grains), so $R_{\rm sub}$
inherits real angle-dependence through the same first-principles geometry
this project already computes, with no separate fitted correction and no
double-counting. The second mechanism (Eddington-ratio/self-shadowing) is
a real, still-missing piece of physics -- no citable per-object formula
was found in either source, only population-level slope shifts -- and is
explicitly excluded from this pass, left as a documented simplification
for a later improvement. Full parameter list in
\texttt{src/demiurge/parameters/registry.py}
(\texttt{quasar\_continuum.dissociation\_radius.*}).

\subsection{Gas-phase metal depletion onto dust}
\label{sec:depletion}

\texttt{dust.depletion} ties gas-phase metal abundances to a volume
element's own total (gas+dust) metal budget -- needed for the not-yet-built
CLOUDY-based ionization emulator's metallicity self-consistency, registered
ahead of that consumer. \citet{GutkinEtAl2016} parameterize depletion with a
single free dust-to-metal mass ratio $\xi_d \in [0, 1]$ (solar baseline
$\xi_{d,\odot}=0.36$): each REFRACTORY element's depletion factor
$f_{\rm dpl}^i(\xi_d)$ is piecewise-linear through $(0,0)$, their own
measured Table 1 value at $\xi_d=\xi_{d,\odot}$, and $(1,1)$ -- their own
stated boundary condition. Non-refractory elements (nitrogen, the one this
project currently registers; also He/Ne/S/Ar per their Table 1 footnote)
have $f_{\rm dpl}^i=0$ for EVERY $\xi_d$, not interpolated at all -- an
easy mistake this project's own implementation made once (nitrogen briefly
ramped toward full depletion at high $\xi_d$, caught by a unit test and
verified against the real paper text before fixing, not just patched to
pass). $\xi_d \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(0, 1)$ for general gas; GCB16's own
model grid samples only $0.1/0.3/0.5$ for tractability, not because other
values are unphysical, so the full range is registered.

The identical mechanism extends to AGN narrow-line-region gas via
$\xi_d^{\rm NLR}$ \citep[built on][own AGN-NLR CLOUDY grid]{FeltreEtAl2016},
drawn independently of general $\xi_d$ (physically distinct gas).
\citet{VidalGarciaEtAl2024} fix $\xi_d^{\rm NLR}=0.3$ in their fiducial AGN
models and explore $\mathrm{Uniform}(0.1, 0.5)$ when fitting it, noting a
real degeneracy with $\log U_s^{\rm NLR}$ and $Z_{\rm gas}^{\rm NLR}$ in
their own line-ratio fitting. Since both papers treat $0.3$ as the
physically-preferred value rather than an arbitrary grid midpoint,
$\xi_d^{\rm NLR} \sim \mathrm{TruncatedNormal}(0.3, 0.1, [0,1])$ is
registered instead of a flat Uniform -- centered on that value, with their
own explored range at roughly $\pm 2\sigma$; their degeneracy caveat comes
from INVERSE line-ratio fitting, which may not transfer to this project's
forward generation from priors (noted, not yet resolved). Full parameter
list in \texttt{src/demiurge/parameters/registry.py}
(\texttt{dust.depletion.*}).

\section{Blending}
\label{sec:blending}

\texttt{blending.continuum.blend\_continua} composes
Section~\ref{sec:galaxy-continuum}'s stellar continuum and
Section~\ref{sec:quasar-continuum}'s AGN continuum into one composite mock,
weighted by \texttt{blending.quasar\_frac} -- the fraction of
\emph{bolometric} luminosity (each channel's own native, full-spectrum
integral) associated with the quasar's accretion physics/SED. This
generalizes an initially simpler proposal
($\texttt{quasar\_frac}\times\text{quasar} +
(1-\texttt{quasar\_frac})\times\text{galaxy}$, applied directly to each
channel's raw output) that implicitly assumed both channels were already on
a common flux scale; they are not, since each is independently normalized
from its own real physical parameters (galaxy: total stellar mass; quasar:
black hole mass $\times$ Eddington ratio, tied to $\dot m L_{\rm Edd}$) with
no imposed relation between the two.

Mechanism -- a symmetric, total-luminosity-preserving rescale:
\begin{align}
    L_{\rm bol,gal} &= \int L_{\nu,\rm gal}\,d\nu \quad\text{(galaxy's own native grid)}\\
    L_{\rm bol,qso} &= \int L_{\nu,\rm qso}\,d\nu \quad\text{(quasar's own native grid, BEFORE reddening)}\\
    L_{\rm bol,total} &= L_{\rm bol,gal} + L_{\rm bol,qso}\\
    c_{\rm gal} &= (1-\texttt{quasar\_frac})\, L_{\rm bol,total}/L_{\rm bol,gal}\\
    c_{\rm qso} &= \texttt{quasar\_frac}\, L_{\rm bol,total}/L_{\rm bol,qso}
\end{align}
with the final composite $c_{\rm gal} L_{\lambda,\rm gal} + c_{\rm qso}
L_{\lambda,\rm qso} T_{\rm torus} T_{\rm hostdisk}$ (reddening, if any,
applied only to the quasar component, AFTER this rescale -- the bolometric
fraction describes the intrinsic accretion-power budget, not how obscured
this particular mock's view of it happens to be). By construction, verified
directly rather than merely asserted (\texttt{tests/blending/}), the
composite's actual achieved bolometric fraction equals
\texttt{quasar\_frac} exactly at every value in $[0,1]$, not only the
edges, and the rescale is singularity-free across the full range (holding
either channel fixed at its own native scale instead moves the singularity
to the opposite edge). One deliberate, flagged consequence:
\texttt{quasar\_frac}$=0$ gives exactly zero quasar contribution but
rescales the galaxy component to $L_{\rm bol,total}$ rather than its own
untouched native output (symmetrically at $\texttt{quasar\_frac}=1$) --
absolute brightness is not preserved at either edge, only composition,
consistent with this project's expectation that the composite will be
renormalized for magnitude invariance downstream regardless.
\texttt{quasar\_frac} itself (\texttt{blending.quasar\_frac}, Tier 3,
$\mathrm{Uniform}(0,1)$) is \texttt{physical=False}: it is a
dataset-construction/generation control, not a property any real observed
spectrum carries a ground-truth label for (a real spectrum's AGN fraction
is a quantity fit \emph{from} it, never supplied as an input).
